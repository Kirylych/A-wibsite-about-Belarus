General Information

Alexander Grigoryevich Lukashenko

The first President of the Republic of Belarus. In our country, he is more than a politician.

Alexander Lukashenko stands at the origins of the formation and development of independent Belarus. For fellow presidents, he is a recognized authority. In defending national interests, he is known for a firm, sometimes tough, but invariably pro-Belarusian position. This is appreciated by citizens of other countries, who place the leader of our state at the top of trust ratings.

As the guarantor of sovereignty, President Lukashenko defends the independence and security of the country as the Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces and Chairman of the Security Council of the Republic of Belarus. And in these positions, the Belarusian leader is unwavering and principled in promoting national interests.

"We are not just building a state. We are building a home where our children and grandchildren will live. And we will do everything to make this home strong and safe."

— Alexander Lukashenko

Geography

The Republic of Belarus is located in the center of Europe, in its eastern part. Major transit routes of the continent pass through the territory of the country, including the main transport corridor of Eurasia from southwest to northeast. The shortest transport routes connecting the Baltic and Black Seas also run here.

📍 Borders and Area

  • Total area: 207,600 km²
  • Border length: 3,617 km
  • Neighbors: Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, Russia, Ukraine
  • Distance to capitals: Vilnius — 215 km, Riga — 470 km, Warsaw — 550 km, Kyiv — 580 km, Moscow — 700 km

⛰️ Terrain

  • Located within the East European Platform
  • Predominantly flat terrain with hills, lowlands, marshes and lakes
  • Lowlands (up to 200 m) — about 70% of territory
  • Lowest point: Neman River valley (80–90 m)
  • Highest point: Dzerzhinsky Hill (345 m)

🌲 Natural Zones

  • North: Belarusian Lake District (glacial relief, hills and ridges)
  • Center: Belarusian Ridge and East Belarusian plateau-like lowland
  • South: Polesie (morainic and aquaglacial plains, often marshy lowlands)

Population

As of 1 January 2026, the population of Belarus was 9,056,080 people. This is about 0.1% of the world population and approximately 1.2% of the European population. Belarus ranks 99th in the world and 7th in the CIS by population.

Places to Visit

💡 Click on any region title or attraction to see its details.

Brest Oblast

  • Brest Hero Fortress — one of the most famous WWII memorials in Belarus, awarded the title "Hero Fortress" in 1965
  • Belovezhskaya Pushcha National Park — a UNESCO World Heritage Site, one of Europe's largest primeval forests
  • Kosovo Palace and Park Ensemble — a 19th-century neo-Gothic palace known as "the little Versailles of Belarus"
  • Ruzhany Sapieha Palace — the ruins of a historic palace
  • Pripyat River Cruises — the only river cruise experience in Belarus

Vitebsk Oblast

  • Braslav Lakes National Park — 267 lakes and 216 bird species
  • Ginkovskaya Cave — the only karst cave in Belarus
  • Kozyany Swamp Nature Reserve — a unique "Mercury-like landscape"
  • Ula Castle Ruins — remains of a 16th-century castle

Gomel Oblast

  • Gomel Palace and Park Ensemble — former palace of the Rumyantsev and Paskevich families
  • Turov — the ancient capital of Polesie
  • Ola River Ecological Trail — opened in 2024
  • Pripyatsky National Park — over 250 bird species
  • Vetka — a historic center of Old Believers

Grodno Oblast

  • Krewo Castle — 14th-century castle, site of the Union of Krewo (1385)
  • Kalozha Church — the oldest surviving Orthodox church in Belarus (12th c.)
  • Mosty Suspension Bridge — the longest in Belarus (193 m)
  • "Free Miller" Tourist Complex — old water mill with traditional food
  • "Korobchitsy" Partisan Camp — authentic WWII recreation

Minsk Oblast

  • Ivyanets — with two remarkable churches and the "Three Bears" sculpture
  • Slutsk — world-famous birthplace of Slutsk sashes
  • Borisov — site of the 1812 Berezina battle
  • Nesvizh Castle — historic palace of the Radziwiłł family

Mogilev Oblast

  • Zhilichy Palace — "the Versailles of Belarus"
  • "Duleby Island" Nature Reserve — unique forest landscape
  • Buynichi — Buynichi Field memorial and zoo
  • "Blue Spring" — one of Europe's largest natural springs
  • Potemkin Palace (Krichev) — 18th-century palace, now a museum
